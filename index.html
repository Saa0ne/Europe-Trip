What changed

This is no longer a road trip. It's a rail trip.

Your original plan assumed a rental car from Berlin with parking strategies for every city. Your actual bookings are a coach to Prague, an ICE to Amsterdam, two FlixBuses, a Eurostar and an ICE home. So the car, fuel and parking layers are gone, and everything is rebuilt around stations, walking distances and city transit.

Baby mode

Built for a 4-month-old on trains.

No car seat needed on this itinerary. What matters instead is lifts, platform gaps, feed timing and how long each transfer really takes.

🚄 Long-leg rules

  • Book a seat with a table on the ICE legs — it doubles as a changing and feeding surface.
  • ICE trains have a baby-change compartment; on FlixBus the onboard toilet does not, so change the baby before boarding and at every rest stop.
  • Feed on departure and on arrival. Pressure is not an issue on trains, but routine is.
  • Keep one small bag with nappies, wipes, a spare outfit and a bottle at your feet, never in the overhead rack.
  • Board early. Reaching your seat with a pram after 40 other people is the single most stressful moment of any rail day.

🛗 Station and pram reality

  • Prague metro: Nádraží Holešovice and Florenc both have lifts, but check the door on the platform — some are step-free only via one exit.
  • Paris: most Métro stations are not step-free. Line 14 and the T3b tram are. Plan around that, or take a taxi.
  • Amsterdam / Zaandam: fully lift-equipped and the flattest city on the route.
  • Brussels-Nord → hotel: flat pavement the whole way, no steps.
  • Cologne Hbf → Hansaring: a 1.3 km flat walk, or one lift-served S-Bahn stop.

🍼 Supplies on the road

  • Pharmacies: Lékárna (CZ), Apotheke (DE), Apotheek (NL), Apotheek/Pharmacie (BE), Pharmacie (FR).
  • Nappies and formula are cheapest in supermarkets: Albert/Rossmann (CZ), dm/Rossmann (DE), Albert Heijn/Kruidvat (NL), Delhaize/Colruyt (BE), Monoprix/Carrefour (FR).
  • Carry your EHIC/GHIC or travel insurance card plus the baby's passport at all times — you cross four borders.
  • Sunday closures are real in Germany and Belgium. Stock up on Saturday.

⏱️ The pacing rule that saves the trip

  • One anchor activity per day. Everything else is optional.
  • A 13:00–15:00 hotel window on every non-travel day, non-negotiable.
  • Sunset is around 20:00 in early September, so evening sightseeing after a nap works beautifully.
  • If two of you are tired and the baby is fine, still stop. The next day is the one you protect.

🏰 Disneyland with a 4-month-old · 10 Sep

  • Tickets: dated and online only, cheapest booked early. Under-3s go free, so you buy two adult tickets and nothing for the baby.
  • Baby Care Center on Main Street, U.S.A. — changing tables, bottle warming, a quiet room to feed. There is a second quiet option at First Aid in the other park. Locate it on arrival, not in a crisis.
  • Rider Switch is free and made for exactly your situation: one of you waits with the baby, then swaps onto the ride without queueing again.
  • There is no daycare anywhere in the resort. The baby is with you all day.
  • Bring your own pram rather than renting — you need it for the RER anyway, and rental prams cannot leave the park.
  • Fantasyland is the whole point at this age: It's a Small World, the carousel, the Disneyland Railroad and the boat rides are all gentle, seated, shaded and often quiet inside.
  • Paths are flat and wide, so the pram is genuinely comfortable here — a rarity on this trip.
  • Leave before the night show. It runs around 22:00 and a 4-month-old plus fireworks plus a 21:00 RER is a bad combination.

✈️ The flight home · BER 09:40 on 14 Sep

  • Bassinet: request it now, not at the airport. Most airlines fit them to bulkhead rows only, with a weight limit around 10–11 kg, and they are allocated first come, first served.
  • Infant ticket: an under-2 travelling on a lap still needs a ticket and a boarding pass. Confirm the baby is on the booking for both flights, not just the first.
  • Liquids: baby milk, formula, expressed milk and water for the baby are exempt from the 100 ml rule at EU security. Carry them separately and declare them. Expect them to be screened.
  • Pram: ask at bag drop for a gate tag so you keep it right up to boarding. Compact prams sometimes go in the cabin, but never assume it.
  • Ears: feed or offer a dummy on the climb and on every descent. This is the one place where cabin pressure genuinely matters, unlike on the trains.
  • Connection: there is no non-stop BER → Bengaluru, so you will change once. Under 90 minutes at a large hub with a pram is tight — ask about a longer layover if that is what you have been given.
  • Documents: passports for all three, plus the baby's visa or OCI paperwork if India requires it for your nationality. Check this weeks ahead, not the night before.
Playbook

Do, don't, and the honest trade-offs.

✅ Do

  • Buy every city's day or multi-day transit pass on arrival, not per ride.
  • Validate FlixBus and RegioJet tickets on the app and screenshot them — coach terminals have poor signal.
  • Be at coach stops 20 minutes early; coaches leave on time and rarely wait.
  • Use the 30 Aug and 4 Sep mornings as slow mornings. They precede long travel.
  • Do Bruges and Ghent on the same day trip. They are 27 minutes apart on the same line.
  • Book Disneyland tickets online for a dated 10 Sep entry, and leave the hotel by 06:45 to be at the gates before opening.
  • Keep 13 Sep completely empty. That is your buffer before the flight, and it is part of the plan.
  • Book the 14 Sep taxi at reception on the 13th, for 06:15. Nine minutes of admin removes the only real risk on flight morning.
  • Request the bassinet and confirm the infant is ticketed on both flights the moment you read this.

❌ Don't

  • Don't attempt Giethoorn. Without a car it eats an entire day for 40 minutes of village.
  • Don't plan sightseeing on 28 Aug beyond a gentle Letná or Stromovka walk — you'll have been travelling for 12 hours.
  • Don't rely on the Paris Métro with the pram for the airport-style luggage days. Take a taxi on 9 and 12 Sep.
  • Don't cut the Gare du Nord buffer. Gates close 30 minutes before departure and they do not reopen.
  • Don't add Antwerp, Rotterdam or Luxembourg. The itinerary is already at its ceiling.
  • Don't stay for the Disneyland night show. It ends around 22:00 and puts you back at the hotel near midnight.
  • Don't plan anything demanding on 11 Sep beyond Trocadéro and Montmartre. Disneyland took the day that used to absorb the rest.
  • Don't book timed museum slots on any travel day.
  • Don't bet flight morning on one early S9. It is now the only line at Grünbergallee, and there is no second train worth waiting for.
  • Don't pack the baby liquids deep in a bag. They come out at security, every time.

The day itself works better than people expect. Getting there is one seated 35-minute RER ride after two short Métro hops, and the station is a five-minute flat walk from both gates. Inside, the paths are wide and flat, there is a Baby Care Center on Main Street, Rider Switch lets you take turns on anything the baby cannot do, and Fantasyland is full of gentle indoor rides that double as nap opportunities. Your baby will not remember a second of it — this one is for you, and that is a perfectly good reason.

It's a fair trade. Résidence Internationale de Paris sits near Porte de Bagnolet with the T3b tram at the door and Métro 3 five minutes away, so Opéra is roughly 20 minutes and Père Lachaise is a 15-minute walk. You lose the ability to walk to the Eiffel Tower; you save a lot of money and you gain a quiet residential base with a garden, which matters more with an infant.
Your exposure is highest on the two FlixBus legs, because coaches have no alternative service and no through-ticketing. If the 7 Sep or 9 Sep bus is cancelled, the fallback is rail: Amsterdam → Brussels is direct on Eurostar or the IC Brussel (roughly 2–3 hours), and Brussels → Paris is direct on Eurostar (about 1h25). Both are expensive walk-up, so keep a card with headroom. The ICE and Eurostar legs are covered by operator rebooking and, in Germany, by delay compensation from 60 minutes. The one leg with no slack behind it is ICE 557 on 13 Sep, because a flight follows it — which is exactly why the 13th is a buffer day and not a sightseeing day.
For a 09:40 long-haul connection with an infant, aim to be inside Terminal 1 by 06:45, which means leaving the hotel at 06:15. That is about three hours, and it is not padding: bag drop with a pram and a gate tag, security with declared baby liquids, and a feed before boarding each take longer than you expect, and BER's morning peak between 06:00 and 08:00 is its busiest window. If your bags are through-checked to Bengaluru you only do this once, which is the whole argument for confirming that at check-in.
Two things, and both work in your favour. The Dresdner Bahn opened, so FEX and RE trains now run direct from BER via Südkreuz to Berlin Hbf — roughly 14 minutes to Südkreuz and 23 to Hbf, instead of the old 39-minute crawl. At the same time the S45 was withdrawn, so any older guide telling you to ride the S45 from the airport to Südkreuz is out of date. The practical consequences: your 28 Aug arrival transfer is much faster than planned, and Grünbergallee on 13–14 Sep is now served by the S9 alone, which is why the airport taxi is worth booking.
